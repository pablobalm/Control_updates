\section*{Selection bar}
A paper is picked only if it clearly does at least one of the following:
introduces a new problem formulation, framework or system-theoretic concept;
brings a mathematical tool into control in a way that changes what can be proved or computed;
proves a fundamental limit, an impossibility result or a tight characterisation, or settles a known open problem;
removes a standing assumption, or changes scalability or the kind of guarantee qualitatively.
Excluded: extensions that reuse the same proof technique, less conservative \ac{LMI} conditions,
known methods (\eg, \ac{MPC} or \ac{RL}) applied to a new plant, and simulation-only claims.
arXiv preprints are assessed from their full text; journal articles from their abstracts only.

Sources: arXiv (eess.SY and math.OC, including cross-lists) and new articles in IEEE TAC, Automatica, SICON,
IEEE TCNS, MCSS, ESAIM:COCV and SIADS, plus control-related articles in SIOPT, Mathematical Programming and

\section{Week ending 6 November 2026}
\label{week:2026-11-06}
\paragraph{Judgement.} A strong week, dominated by exact answers to long-standing structural questions: seven preprints clear the bar, while none of the 42 journal items in the window does. The most important are a global small-time controllability theorem for control-affine systems with drift, in which quadratic Lie brackets are the only remaining obstruction; the existence of approximate equilibria in multiplayer stopping games, which settles the three-player case of a long-open problem; and an exact decidability boundary for distributed synthesis over lossy links, which turns out to coincide with solvability of repeated consensus. The other four are tight characterisations: the possible regret regimes of adaptive \ac{LQG} control, the number of critical points of $H_2$ model reduction, minimal observation times for Grushin heat equations on the whole space, and the claim distributions for which barrier dividend policies are always optimal.

\subsection*{Preprints (full-text assessment)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Small-time controllability of affine systems and quadratic Lie brackets}~\cite{beauchard2026small} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02293}{arXiv:2611.02293}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} control-affine systems $\dot x = f_0(x) + \sum_{j=1}^m u_j f_j(x)$ on a smooth connected manifold, with smooth vector fields and unbounded $L^1$ controls; the property sought is small-time global exact controllability, \ie every state can be steered to every other state in arbitrarily short time. Holomorphic systems on $\Cset^d$ and control of groups of diffeomorphisms are treated as variants.\\
  \emph{Main result:} with $M^j_0 = f_j$, $M^j_k = [M^j_{k-1}, f_0]$ and the brackets $W^j_k = [M^j_{k-1}, M^j_k]$, which are quadratic in $f_j$, the system is small-time globally exactly controllable if the Lie algebra generated by the $M^j_k$ up to chosen orders spans the tangent space at every point, if the flows of every $W^j_k$ can be approximated in arbitrarily short time in both directions, and if the spans of the iterated brackets $\mathrm{ad}^r_{M^j_k} f_0$ are finite dimensional. When all these quadratic brackets vanish the rank condition alone suffices (for linear systems this is the Kalman rank condition). Analogues hold for small-time approximate controllability of compactly supported and of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms, and, under a weaker hypothesis, for holomorphic systems.\\
  \emph{Technique:} vector fields whose flows are small-time approximately reachable are shown to form a Lie algebra, using product formulas for sums and brackets of flows; the key step shows that if $g$ and $\mathrm{ad}^\rho_g f_0$ have this property then so does $\mathrm{ad}^{\rho-1}_g f_0$ (for odd $\rho - 1$), by conjugating a rescaled drift with flows of $g$; induction over $k$ then leaves only the quadratic brackets as hypotheses, and the Rashevski--Chow theorem with a backward-accessibility argument turns approximate into exact controllability.\\
  \emph{What is new:} quadratic brackets were known as obstructions to small-time \emph{local} controllability (Sussmann, 1983; Stefani, 1986; Beauchard and Marbach, 2018); this is a global counterpart, and its key step relaxes the hypotheses of Agrachev's ``good brackets'' (Agrachev, 2024), which allow only positive amplitudes and need a higher iterated bracket to vanish. Recent small-time controllability results for bilinear Schr{\"o}dinger and wave equations (Pozzoli, 2024; Beauchard and Pozzoli, 2025) are reread as instances of the same quadratic-bracket mechanism.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} the condition is only sufficient; the hypothesis that the quadratic brackets themselves are small-time reachable is the hard part and has to be proved case by case; the finite-dimensionality condition is technical; the examples are hand computations in dimension three and four, and the discussion of partial differential equations is heuristic.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a structural theorem that changes what can be proved about small-time global controllability with drift, isolating quadratic brackets as the only obstruction once a rank condition holds; confidence high (full text).
  \item \textbf{Multiplayer Stopping Games with a Conditionally Atomless Filtration}~\cite{simon2026multiplayer} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07184}{arXiv:2611.07184}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} discrete-time stopping games with $n \ge 2$ players: at every stage each player decides, possibly at random, whether to stop, and an adapted payoff process with an integrable supremum gives each player's payoff as a function of the first stage at which someone stops and of who stops then; no ordering or sign conditions are imposed on the payoffs.\\
  \emph{Main result:} every such game has an $\epsilon$-equilibrium for every $\epsilon > 0$ if the filtration is conditionally atomless, meaning that every event can be split into smaller events with strictly smaller conditional probability given any finite stage (repeated fair coin tosses are an example); every three-player game has an $\epsilon$-equilibrium for every $\epsilon > 0$ without this condition.\\
  \emph{Technique:} after reducing to bounded, finitely valued and generic payoffs, the proof splits on whether a matrix of payoffs from single-player stopping is close to a Q-matrix; if it is, sunspot equilibria of quitting games are imitated, with events generated by the rich filtration replacing the public randomisation device; if it is not, the two-player stopping-time construction, based on a stochastic version of Ramsey's theorem and approximating trees, is extended to $n$ players; for three players, periodic equilibria of Flesch, Thuijsman and Vrieze (1997) make the richness condition unnecessary.\\
  \emph{What is new:} approximate equilibria were known to exist for two players (Shmaya and Solan, 2004), for multiplayer quitting games with a public randomisation device (Solan and Solan, 2020), and for multiplayer stopping games under ordering conditions on the payoffs (Hamad{\`e}ne, Hdhiri and Mannai, 2024); the general multiplayer case had been open, and the insight that the filtration's own randomness can serve as the correlation device is new.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} the authors do not know whether the result holds for four or more players without the richness condition; discrete time only (three-player games in continuous time can lack approximate equilibria); the proof is purely existential and long; the fetched text ended in Section 4, so the proof of the three-player theorem and the discussion were not read.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} settles a known open problem on sequential decisions, completely for three players and for any number of players under a mild richness condition, with a genuinely new idea; confidence high (full text up to Section 4).
  \item \textbf{Distributed LTL Synthesis under Arbitrary Message Adversaries}~\cite{bollig2026distributed} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04561}{arXiv:2611.04561}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} two processes run in synchronous rounds; in each round they read inputs, exchange messages carrying everything they know, and produce outputs; at least one of the two messages is delivered per round, and a message adversary is a set of allowed infinite sequences of the three possible communication graphs. Synthesis asks whether some distributed algorithm, \ie a map from local views to outputs, satisfies a given \ac{LTL} specification on every execution the adversary allows.\\
  \emph{Main result:} synthesis is undecidable when every sequence of graphs is allowed, even though messages carry full histories; for an $\omega$-regular adversary $A$, synthesis is decidable if and only if the image $\pi(A) \subseteq [0,1]$ of its graph sequences under iterated chromatic subdivision is nowhere dense, which holds if and only if consensus can be solved repeatedly in bounded time under $A$; in the decidable case a procedure decides realisability and constructs a realising algorithm from a deterministic parity automaton for $A$.\\
  \emph{Technique:} undecidability by reduction from two-counter machines, with the counter values stored in the processes' uncertainty about which graphs occurred rather than in hidden inputs; the topology of the view complex, whose edges are trisected in every round, links decidability to nowhere density; decidability by reduction to finite symbolic trees of view-complex components that are checked in monadic second-order logic (Rabin's theorem); the key results are machine-checked in Lean.\\
  \emph{What is new:} the closest work (B{\'e}rard, Bollig, Bouyer, F{\"u}gger and Sznajder, 2022) assumed acknowledgements, so that both processes know each round's graph, and found decidability exactly when silent rounds are uniformly bounded; without acknowledgements even consensus behaves differently, and both the exact boundary and its equivalence with repeated bounded-time consensus are new; the result refines the classical undecidability of distributed synthesis (Pnueli and Rosner, 1990).\\
  \emph{Limitations:} two processes only; adversaries must be $\omega$-regular; no complexity bound for the decision procedure appears in the parts read, and no concrete decidable adversary is worked out there; the text read stopped in Section 6, before the conclusion.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a tight characterisation, an exact decidability boundary, for controller synthesis over unreliable communication, with an unexpected link to consensus; confidence high (full text up to Section 6).
  \item \textbf{Expected Minimax Regret Rates for Adaptive LQG Control on Fixed Parameter Sets}~\cite{liu2026expected} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01739}{arXiv:2611.01739}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} discrete-time partially observed linear-Gaussian systems whose matrices depend affinely on an unknown parameter in a known compact ball, with unknown process and measurement noise covariances in known bounded sets; every model is stabilisable and detectable; quadratic cost with known positive definite weights; causal output-feedback policies are compared, in the worst case over the family, with the optimal Kalman-filter-plus-feedback controller that knows the model.\\
  \emph{Main result:} the minimax expected regret over horizon $T$ is either bounded, $\Theta(\log T)$, $\Theta(T^{1-1/\rho})$ with a rational $\rho > 1$, or $\Theta(T)$; the case is decided by how fast an information measure (differences of residual covariances, plus differences of input responses weighted by differences of optimal controllers) can vanish relative to the distance between optimal controllers, which defines $\rho$; for real algebraic data a terminating procedure returns the case and the exact exponent before any data are collected; a causal policy attains the rates without a common stabilising controller being supplied; for costs on outputs and inputs the exponent is at most $1/2$.\\
  \emph{Technique:} lower bounds from a two-model argument that bounds the Kullback--Leibler divergence between the observation laws of two models by the regret they force; upper bounds from a certainty-equivalent policy run in doubling blocks with probing amplitudes tuned to $\rho$, least-squares refits and restarts; rationality and computability of $\rho$ from semialgebraic geometry (quantifier elimination and rational exponents of semialgebraic functions of one variable).\\
  \emph{What is new:} earlier work gave rates for particular algorithms or classes, such as $\sqrt{T}$ for fully observed \ac{LQR} (Simchowitz and Foster, 2020) and logarithmic regret under structural assumptions for partial observation (Lale et al., 2020); the author's own earlier classification was local, on shrinking neighbourhoods of fully observed structured \ac{LQR}; this is a complete and exact classification on a fixed family with partial observation and unknown noise covariances.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} a long single-author paper whose proofs are in appendices that were cut off in the text read; constants are not explicit; the policy needs $\rho$ and the horizon as inputs; compact affine families only; no simulations, and the examples are scalar or two-dimensional.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a tight characterisation of achievable regret in adaptive \ac{LQG} control, with a hierarchy of regimes read off from algebraic data; confidence medium (full text; appendices not reached).
  \item \textbf{Porteous Meets Walsh: Counting Critical Points for $H_2$-Norm Model Order Reduction Using Algebraic Geometry Behind Multiparameter Eigenvalue Problems}~\cite{lagauw2026porteous} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00258}{arXiv:2611.00258}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} optimal $H_2$ approximation of a stable, strictly proper single-input single-output continuous-time transfer function of order $N$ by one of order $n < N$; by Walsh's theorem the stationary points solve a polynomial system that is linear in part of the unknowns, so it can be written as a rectangular multiparameter eigenvalue problem.\\
  \emph{Main result:} if the full model has $N$ distinct poles and no pole--zero cancellation, the number of complex critical points (up to scaling) is at most $\sum_{j=0}^{n} \binom{N-n-1+j}{j} 2^j$, with equality and simple solutions for generic models; this gives 49 instead of the B{\'e}zout bound 1458 for $(N,n) = (6,2)$, and $2^{n+1}-1$ when $n = N-1$; with repeated poles spurious solutions are still excluded, but the formula is only an upper bound.\\
  \emph{Technique:} critical points are identified with the rank-drop locus of a polynomial matrix in the coefficients of the reduced denominator; a dimension count on the variety of data and critical points, shown to be an irreducible complete intersection, gives generic finiteness and reducedness, and the Thom--Porteous formula for degeneracy loci gives the degree.\\
  \emph{What is new:} only an upper bound for co-order one was known (Hanzon, Maciejowski and Chou, 2007), generic finiteness was proved separately (Hanzon, Peeters and Bleylevens, 2012), and the eigenvalue-problem formulation is the authors' own (Lagauw, Agudelo and De Moor, 2023); an exact count for all $(N,n)$, and the use of intersection theory to obtain it, are new.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} the count is of complex solutions, whereas the number of real, stable stationary points depends on the data; single-input single-output and generic simple poles only; the sections on discrete time, examples and conclusions were cut off in the text read, and no numerical check of the count was visible.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} brings intersection theory into control to compute exactly a quantity that sets the size of any algebraic method certifying the global optimum, and gives the first exact count; confidence high (full text up to Section 4).
  \item \textbf{Observability and stabilizability for Grushin-Type parabolic equations in the whole space}~\cite{huang2026observability} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05821}{arXiv:2611.05821}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} the Grushin heat equation $\partial_t u - \Delta_x u - |x|^{2\gamma}\Delta_y u = \mathbf{1}_E h$ on $\Rset^n \times \Rset^m$ with $\gamma > 0$, controlled from a product set $E = E_1 \times E_2$ of positive measure; null controllability (equivalently observability), and exponential or complete stabilisability by bounded feedback.\\
  \emph{Main result:} for every $\gamma > 0$ the equation is exponentially, equivalently completely, stabilisable from $E$ if and only if $E_1$ and $E_2$ are thick, so stabilisability does not see the degeneracy; for $0 < \gamma < 1$ thickness is also equivalent to null controllability in every time; for $\gamma = 1$ and $E = E_1 \times \Rset^m$ with $E_1$ thick, null controllability holds exactly for $T > a^2/(2n)$, where $a$ is the essential distance from $E_1$ to the degeneracy set $\{x = 0\}$; for $\gamma > 1$ (with $n = 1$) it fails in every time if $a > 0$, and otherwise depends on how densely $E_1$ fills small neighbourhoods of the origin, with sufficient and necessary density conditions that do not match.\\
  \emph{Technique:} a partial Fourier transform in $y$ and rescaling reduce the problem to a family of anharmonic oscillators (harmonic for $\gamma = 1$); a spectral inequality uniform in the frequency, proved by propagation of smallness, feeds the Lebeau--Robbiano strategy; the sharp time for $\gamma = 1$ comes from explicit Gaussian (Hermite) computations, and the failures for $\gamma > 1$ from coherent-state constructions (Koenig, 2017).\\
  \emph{What is new:} the dependence on $\gamma$ and the minimal times were known on bounded domains and strips (Beauchard, Cannarsa and Guglielmi, 2014; Beauchard, Miller and Morancey, 2015), and thickness characterises null controllability of the ordinary heat equation on the whole space (Egidi and Veseli\'{c}, 2018; Wang, Wang, Zhang and Zhang, 2019); this paper gives the whole-space, thick-set picture with an exact minimal time in every dimension, and shows that stabilisability and controllability separate: the former is the same for all $\gamma$, the latter is not.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} product sets or strips only; the strongly degenerate case $\gamma > 1$ is restricted to $n = 1$ and its minimal time is left open; the proofs of the main theorems were cut off in the text read.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} tight characterisations, an exact minimal time and an if-and-only-if criterion for stabilisability, for a model degenerate parabolic equation, obtained with a new frequency-uniform spectral estimate; confidence medium (full text; proofs not reached).
  \item \textbf{When are dividend barriers optimal? A distributional characterization in renewal risk models}~\cite{miao2026dividend} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00827}{arXiv:2611.00827}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} de Finetti's dividend problem in a renewal (Sparre Andersen) risk model: premiums at a constant rate, independent claims at renewal times with a fixed law of interclaim times (continuous hazard and bounded mean residual waiting time), unrestricted dividend payments, discounting and ruin at negative surplus, over a finite or infinite horizon; the controller observes claim times and sizes, and a barrier may depend on time and on the time since the last claim.\\
  \emph{Main result:} among claim-size laws with a continuous nondecreasing hazard rate, a globally optimal barrier exists for every premium rate above the net-profit level, every discount rate and every horizon if and only if claims are exponential; for every other law in this class there is a premium and discount pair for which no barrier is optimal at any sufficiently long horizon or on the infinite horizon, because paying out a small surplus at once is optimal while a larger surplus should partly be retained, so the value function is not concave in the surplus.\\
  \emph{Technique:} dynamic programming in integral form, conditioning on the first claim, so that no smoothness of the value function is needed; for exponential claims, memorylessness bounds the marginal value of capital, which gives concavity by induction and an explicit optimal barrier; for other laws, a premium close to the net-profit level and an identity for the killed surplus process force immediate payment at small surplus, while a short wait beats immediate payment at a larger one.\\
  \emph{What is new:} barrier strategies were known to fail in particular renewal examples (Albrecher and Hartinger, 2006) and to be optimal under sufficient conditions such as completely monotone jump densities for spectrally negative L{\'e}vy models (Loeffen, 2008); this paper turns the question into an exact distributional characterisation within a broad class.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} the equivalence is confined to claim laws with increasing hazard and needs a uniform bound on the mean residual waiting time; the parameters for which barriers fail have a small safety loading; the numerical comparison of exponential and Gamma claims and the end of the converse proof were cut off in the text read.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a tight characterisation of when the simplest feedback policy is optimal in a classical singular stochastic control problem; confidence medium (full text up to Section 5).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Week ending 30 October 2026}
\label{week:2026-10-30}
\paragraph{Judgement.} A good week for mathematical control theory: four preprints clear the bar, each with an exact answer to a sharply posed question, while none of the 50 new journal articles does. The strongest are an if-and-only-if gain region for \ac{PID} regulation of a broad class of uncertain nonlinear plants, resting on a new Markus--Yamabe-type theorem, and an explicit length minimiser in a Carnot group that is not $C^2$, which shows that the known smoothness of geodesics in rank-two Carnot groups cannot be pushed beyond step four. \subsection*{Preprints (full-text assessment)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Necessary and Sufficient Criteria for Global PID Regulation of Nonlinear Uncertain System}~\cite{zhao2026necessary} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32703}{arXiv:2610.32703}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} second-order plants $\dot x_1 = x_2$, $\dot x_2 = f(x_1,x_2,u)$ with $f$ unknown and $C^1$, known only through the one-sided bounds $\partial f/\partial x_1 \le L_1$, $\partial f/\partial x_2 \le L_2$ and $\partial f/\partial u \ge 1$ (no growth or global Lipschitz condition); one \ac{PID} controller with fixed gains must regulate every plant in this class to any constant set-point from any initial state.\\
  \emph{Main result:} a gain triple works for the whole class if and only if it regulates the single extremal linear plant $f = L_1x_1 + L_2x_2 + u$, \ie if and only if $k_i > 0$, $k_p > L_1$ and $(k_p - L_1)(k_d - L_2) > k_i$; the same region is exact for multi-input plants that are affine in the input, with symmetric Jacobians bounded by $L_1 I$ and $L_2 I$ and input matrix $B = B^\top \succeq I$, whereas without the symmetry no fixed diagonal gains regulate the class; inside the region global exponential stability can fail (the example $f = -x_1^3$).\\
  \emph{Technique:} necessity follows from the extremal linear plant and the Routh--Hurwitz test; sufficiency rests on a new structured Markus--Yamabe theorem for the third-order closed-loop chain, proved with a planar storage function obtained from a viscosity solution of a Hamilton--Jacobi equation, a quadratic correction, mollification and LaSalle's invariance principle.\\
  \emph{What is new:} earlier work of Zhao and Guo (2017, 2022) gave only sufficient gain sets for non-affine plants, and the exact region only for a subclass affine in the velocity; since the Markus--Yamabe property fails in dimension three in general, closing the gap required a structured version of it, which is the paper's main new tool.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} second-order plants, constant set-points and exactly known derivative bounds; the multi-input result needs symmetric Jacobians; sampling, delays and input saturation are not treated; the paper is purely analytical, with no simulations.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a tight characterisation for an infinite-dimensional uncertainty class that reduces to a single linear test, obtained with a genuinely new tool; confidence high (full text).
  \item \textbf{Carnot geodesics with H{\"o}lder controls}~\cite{cavallucci2026carnot} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38481}{arXiv:2610.38481}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} length minimisers among horizontal curves for a rank-two sub-Riemannian structure on $\Rset^4$ with orthonormal frame $X = \partial_x$, $Y = \partial_y + x\,\partial_z + P^2\,\partial_w$, $P = z - \tfrac{2}{5}xy$; the generated Lie algebra is stratified of step five, so the structure lifts, with the same controls and norms, to a rank-two Carnot group of step five.\\
  \emph{Main result:} an explicit curve through the origin, with controls $(\tfrac{3}{2}|t|^{1/2}, 1)$, minimises length for $|t| \le 10^{-8}$; its arc-length parametrisation is $C^{1,1/2}$ and not $C^{1,\alpha}$ for any $\alpha > 1/2$, so it is not $C^2$, and this transfers to the Carnot group; a family announced for a companion paper gives rank-two Carnot groups of step at most $2n+5$ with minimisers that are only $C^{1,1/(2(n+1))}$.\\
  \emph{Technique:} minimality is proved by an approximate calibration built separately for each competitor that is no longer than the curve: from the maximum $\mu$ of $|P|$ along the competitor a $C^1$ potential is built whose increment is at least $L(\gamma) - 5000\mu$ and at most $L(\eta) - (10^8/768)\mu$, a contradiction; the Carnot lift uses Palais's integrability theorem.\\
  \emph{What is new:} smoothness of minimisers was known for rank-two Carnot groups of step at most four, and earlier non-smooth examples include a $C^2$ but not $C^3$ minimiser with a boundary singularity (Chitour et al., 2025); here regularity is lost in the interior of the curve, already at the level of second derivatives and in a Carnot group, so the step-four threshold is sharp, and the loss is invisible to blow-ups.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} one explicit example on a very short interval; the general family and its Carnot lift are deferred to a companion article; whether the minimiser is normal or abnormal is not stated; how it relates to interior singularities found earlier in other real-analytic structures could not be checked in detail; the central open question, $C^1$ regularity of all minimisers, is untouched.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} an impossibility result for the regularity problem of sub-Riemannian geodesics, \ie of length-optimal controls, that is sharp in the step; confidence medium (full text; the general family is only announced).
  \item \textbf{Performance Gaps Between Open-Loop, Certainty-Equivalent, and Optimal Control Under Action-Dependent Observations}~\cite{maynardzhang2026performance} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33412}{arXiv:2610.33412}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} a scalar linear-Gaussian plant $x_{t+1} = Ax_t + Bu_t + w_t$, open-loop stable ($|A| < 1$), with bilinear measurements $y_t = u_t C x_t + z_t$, so the input sets the measurement gain, and a quadratic cost over horizon $T$; three policies are compared: the optimal one, certainty equivalence (full-information gains applied to the Kalman estimate) and the best open-loop policy.\\
  \emph{Main result:} an information-strength parameter $q$, a signal-to-noise ratio of the measurements under the certainty-equivalent gain, has thresholds $q_1 < q_2 < q_3$: for $q < q_1$ no policy beats open-loop control by more than $O(1)$; for $q \in (q_2, q_3)$ the optimal policy beats open-loop control by $\Omega(T)$ while certainty equivalence stays within $O(1)$ of it, so certainty equivalence is suboptimal by a margin linear in the horizon; above a fourth threshold $q_4$ certainty equivalence itself gains $\Omega(T)$ over open-loop control.\\
  \emph{Technique:} the regret against the open-loop benchmark is written as a sum over time of an information credit (the reduction of next-step estimation variance) minus a penalty for deviating from the certainty-equivalent input; the $O(1)$ bounds follow by telescoping, the certainty-equivalent bounds from moment estimates of a multiplicative estimate process, and the optimal policy, which is never computed, is bounded from below by an explicit intermittent probing policy.\\
  \emph{What is new:} Sattar et al.\ (2025) showed that the separation principle fails for quadratic control with bilinear observations, but not how the loss scales with the horizon; this paper gives the first such scaling regimes, a quantitative form of the dual effect of Bar-Shalom and Tse (1974).\\
  \emph{Limitations:} scalar, open-loop stable plants only; certainty equivalence is not characterised for $q$ between $q_3$ and $q_4$; the optimal policy is only bounded, not characterised; no vector or unstable case.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a fundamental limit of certainty-equivalent control when actions shape the information, with an explicit regime where its loss grows linearly in time; confidence medium (full text; scalar setting).
  \item \textbf{Verifying Control Invariance of H-Polytopes is coNP-Complete}~\cite{villanueva2026verifying} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36773}{arXiv:2610.36773}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} discrete-time linear systems $x^+ = Ax + Bu$ with a polytopic input set, given by inequalities or by vertices, and a candidate polytope given by inequalities; rational data, with the state and input dimensions part of the input.\\
  \emph{Main result:} deciding whether the polytope is control invariant is coNP-complete, and stays hard for controllable pairs, full-dimensional polytopes and $m \le n$; consequently, computing the maximal control invariant subset of a polytope in inequality form is coNP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions.\\
  \emph{Technique:} a short reduction from deciding whether a cube lies in the convex hull of given points (coNP-complete; Freund and Orlin, 1985), via a nilpotent two-block system whose input set is a simplex; membership in coNP via a vertex of the polytope plus a Farkas certificate.\\
  \emph{What is new:} for polytopes given by their vertices, control invariance can be checked in polynomial time by linear programming (Gutman and Cwikel, 1986); for the inequality form used in most \ac{MPC} terminal-set computations no complexity classification was available.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} a short note whose reduction leans on known polytope-containment hardness; deterministic discrete-time systems only; robust invariance, continuous time and fixed dimension are not treated; no experiments.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} an impossibility (complexity) result that explains why exact control-invariant-set computations in inequality form scale badly; confidence medium (full text; its weight comes from settling a basic question rather than from new technique).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Preprints (full-text assessment)}
Full text could not be retrieved this week (see Coverage): every entry below rests on the abstract and on short excerpts of the HTML version exposed by the search index, so no confidence rating exceeds medium.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Exact controllability and rapid stabilization of linear difference delay equations and one-dimensional hyperbolic systems}~\cite{chitour2026exact} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31225}{arXiv:2610.31225}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} controlled linear difference delay equations with state space $L^r$, and one-dimensional linear hyperbolic systems that reduce to such equations along characteristics.\\
  \emph{Main result:} a Hautus-type frequency-domain condition, already known to be sufficient for exact controllability in $L^r$, is proved necessary for every $r \in (1,+\infty)$; the same condition is shown to suffice for rapid stabilization in $L^2$ (closed-loop exponential decay at any prescribed rate); for the hyperbolic systems this gives a necessary and sufficient frequency-domain test for exact controllability in $L^r$, $r \in [1,+\infty)$.\\
  \emph{Technique:} the delay system is rewritten as an evolution equation on a suitable Banach space so that duality arguments apply; the criterion itself is of Hautus--Yamamoto (realization-theoretic) type.\\
  \emph{What is new:} Chitour, Fueyo, Mazanti and Sigalotti (2023) proved necessity only in $L^1$ and stated the $L^r$ case as a conjecture, which this paper answers; it also derives rapid stabilization from the same criterion.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} linear, constant-coefficient systems; the precise hypotheses of the main theorems, and how the stabilizing feedback is constructed, could not be checked.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} settles a stated open problem and gives a tight characterisation; confidence medium (abstract and indexed excerpts only, full text unreachable).
  \item \textbf{Necessary and Sufficient Conditions for Adaptive Stabilizability of Continuous-Time Markov Jump Linear Stochastic Systems}~\cite{guo2026necessary} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34197}{arXiv:2610.34197}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} continuous-time Markov jump linear stochastic systems with known candidate modes but an unobserved Markov parameter process, so any stabilizing law must adapt; further regularity assumptions are not spelled out in the abstract.\\
  \emph{Main result:} global adaptive stabilizability holds if and only if the coupled Riccati equations that characterise stabilizability with the mode observed are solvable, \ie the weakest possible structural condition is the non-adaptive one.\\
  \emph{Technique:} not visible from the abstract beyond the coupled Riccati equations through which the condition is stated.\\
  \emph{What is new:} Fang and Loparo (2002) characterised non-adaptive mean-square stabilizability through coupled Riccati equations, and Xue and Guo (2001) showed that in discrete time adaptive stabilizability requires different coupled Riccati equations; here the two conditions coincide, so in continuous time not observing the mode costs nothing for stabilizability.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} single-author preprint; the regularity assumptions, and so the source of the gap with discrete time, could not be checked; nothing on transient performance or on how the adaptive law is built.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a tight characterisation that also exposes a basic difference between continuous and discrete time; confidence medium (abstract only, full text unreachable).
  \item \textbf{Observability from measurable sets for strongly coupled parabolic systems}~\cite{chen2026observability} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31876}{arXiv:2610.31876}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} linear parabolic systems with any number of components, a constant coercive diffusion matrix that need not be diagonalizable, constant coupling and observation matrices, and observation on an arbitrary space-time set of positive measure.\\
  \emph{Main result:} the system is observable from such a set if and only if a generalized Kalman rank condition holds at every Dirichlet eigenvalue.\\
  \emph{Technique:} a Remez-type inequality for exponential polynomials, combined with a spectral inequality and the Lebeau--Robbiano strategy.\\
  \emph{What is new:} Fu, Wang, Yu and Zhu (2026) handled two strongly coupled equations observed through one component; Kalman-type conditions for coupled parabolic systems were known for open control sets (Ammar Khodja, Benabdallah, Dupaix and Gonz{\'a}lez-Burgos, 2009), and measurable-set observability for a single heat equation (Apraiz, Escauriaza, Wang and Zhang, 2014).\\
  \emph{Limitations:} constant coefficients only, so space- or time-dependent couplings are not covered; whether the observability constant is explicit could not be checked.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a tight characterisation that closes the measurable-set case for constant-coefficient coupled systems; confidence medium (abstract only, full text unreachable).
  \item \textbf{Ergodic Linear-Quadratic Optimal Control with Stochastic Coefficients}~\cite{lin2026ergodic} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30219}{arXiv:2610.30219}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} long-run average (ergodic) \ac{LQ} control of linear stochastic systems whose coefficients are themselves random processes.\\
  \emph{Main result:} a stochastic ergodic \ac{HJB} equation in which the ergodic constant becomes a random process $\lambda$; the equation is well posed, and $\lambda$ gives the optimal ergodic cost for a broad class of averaging kernels, including normalized finite-horizon and discounted costs; with random-periodic coefficients a random-periodic solution exists and all admissible kernels give the same optimal cost; the optimal ergodic costs are stable under perturbations of the coefficients.\\
  \emph{Technique:} not visible from the abstract and the excerpts returned by the search.\\
  \emph{What is new:} Guatteri and Masiero (2009) treated stationary stochastic coefficients and Wu and Zhang (2026) random-periodic ones, each with a deterministic ergodic constant; letting the ergodic constant itself be a random process is a new formulation, and one equation then covers several averaging criteria at once.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} \ac{LQ} structure only; the standing assumptions on the coefficients could not be checked; no examples are mentioned.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a new problem formulation for ergodic control under random coefficients; confidence medium (abstract only, full text unreachable).
  \item \textbf{Dual Effect and Certainty Equivalence Revisited for a Linear-Gaussian Dual Control Problem}~\cite{wang2026dual} (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30034}{arXiv:2610.30034}).\\
  \emph{Setting:} a scalar linear-Gaussian dual control problem, with stage costs restricted to those that the certainty-equivalent input minimizes.\\
  \emph{Main result:} a no-dual-effect notion stated on the Bellman objective rather than on the estimator (``Bellman-side'' no dual effect), under which certainty equivalence is optimal for a Bellman step even when the classical no-dual-effect conditions fail; under mild regularity this property is characterised by a heat equation, which identifies the class of such cost-to-go functions and splits any cost-to-go into a no-dual-effect part and a dual-effect part.\\
  \emph{Technique:} analysis of a single Bellman recursion, reducing the requirement that the expected cost-to-go not depend on the current input to a heat equation.\\
  \emph{What is new:} Bar-Shalom and Tse (1974) tied certainty equivalence to the absence of a dual effect, defined through the effect of the control on future estimation uncertainty; this paper moves the notion to the Bellman objective and characterises it exactly.\\
  \emph{Limitations:} scalar model and a restricted class of stage costs; no multivariable extension; the abstract does not say whether the decomposition helps compute dual controllers.\\
  \emph{Verdict:} a new system-theoretic concept with a sharp characterisation, on a deliberately simple model; confidence medium (abstract only, full text unreachable).
\end{enumerate}
